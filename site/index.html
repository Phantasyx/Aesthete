Throughline

This is a small example of a system with one clear rule. You connect a source to a gauge. A piece can be added only where your own line is still open, and only if it faces that opening. The line is finished when every opening meets another opening and the gauge is on the line.

Your line to move

The open end beside the source needs a piece that faces it. Turn a piece until it matches, then click the gold mark.

The line is sealed.

The wheel on the left is the source. The dial on the right is the gauge. The gold mark shows the side the next piece has to open. Copper is the first line. Teal is the second line, when two people share the board.

  1. Choose a piece that opens toward the gold mark.
  2. Click the marked cell. The open end moves to whatever the new piece leaves uncovered.
  3. Open the valve when the line runs all the way to the gauge and nothing is left open.

Pieces

Finish

Opening the valve while something is still open loses the line. If two people are playing, the other person wins.

How the line works

Each player joins two fixed ends. The source opens toward the board, and the gauge opens back toward the pipes. The pieces are caps, elbows, straights, and tees. A finished line has no bare end. That closed path is the throughline. If a pipe runs off the board, that opening can never be closed, and the line cannot be finished.

Why it is here

PhantasyX builds custom software. Throughline is here to show a small piece of interaction craft. The rule is visible, the next move is marked, and the board never leaves your browser. One person can play alone. Two people can pass the same device.

A new board each time

New board deals a different route from the source to the gauge. Each route can be finished. Watch a finished line plays one solution, so you can see the rule close without having to find it yourself.
